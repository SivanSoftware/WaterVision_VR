# Mechanical Reverence

A visual philosophy honoring the gear — humanity's oldest signal for *the controllable*.

## Philosophy

A gear is not decoration. It is a covenant between user and system: *turn me, and something happens.* For three millennia the toothed wheel has carried that promise — in water mills, in clocks, in the first calculating engines, in the icons we now poke through holograms. To draw a gear is therefore to participate in the longest visual lineage of human-made causality. The gear says, with no words: *this is a place where you act.*

The form must be **meticulously crafted** — no wobble, no smear, no concession to convenience. Eight teeth, evenly spaced, each one the rotational mirror of the next to within a fraction of a degree. The pitch circle (where the teeth meet the body) is a perfect circle; the addendum circle (the tooth tips) is a perfect circle. The teeth themselves are rectangular at the icon scale, not chamfered — Brutalist honesty, the gear admitting it is *manufactured*, not grown. Every edge is the product of **painstaking attention** to geometric precision.

Material is monochrome white. Not chrome, not gradient-blessed, not shadow-flattered — flat, unornamented, *signal*. The white must read against any background (dark glow, light wall, fingertip silhouette) with the indifference of a road sign. Negative space is the central hub hole — a small perfect circle, the bearing pin missing, the axis declared and then withheld. Padding around the gear breathes ~10% to keep the silhouette from feeling cramped; the bounding box is the wrist's territory, not the gear's. Composition is **rotationally symmetric** — the gear reads correctly at any 45° rotation of the wrist, an essential property for a wrist-mounted control whose orientation cannot be predicted.

Scale is small but uncompromising. At 256×256 the gear must hold up under bilinear minification down to 32×32 in the headset's pixel grid. This means: tooth width to gap width must be 50/50 (any narrower and antialiasing eats the teeth); inner radius must be no more than 78% of outer (any closer and the teeth become bumps); the central hole must be ~18% of the outer radius (a clear visual punctuation, not a pixel-hostage dot). These are not arbitrary numbers — they are the result of **master-level execution** applied to the practical demands of a real render pipeline.

The icon's deepest reference is to the **gear-as-tool-icon** lineage that runs from medieval guild marks through Macintosh's "Control Panel" cog (1984) and into every Settings glyph since. By drawing it cleanly, without flourish, the icon enters that conversation as a peer, not a quotation. A gear is a small monument to the idea that complexity can be *configured*. To depict it with restraint is the highest form of respect.

## Output

Single PNG, 256×256, transparent background, white silhouette, no shadows, no gradients, no glow. Centered. ~10% padding. 8 rectangular teeth. Central hole.
