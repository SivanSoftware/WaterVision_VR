# Restrained Dimension

A discipline of quiet precision. The work asserts itself not through ornament but through the patient calibration of edges, light, and breathing space — an object that rewards close looking and still earns the glance of someone hurrying past.

The composition is a single perfect circle held in clean negative space. Its surface is dark, settled, watch-face quiet — a material thing that absorbs light rather than reflecting it. A single hairline of luminance traces its perimeter, the kind of subtle dimension that takes a master countless attempts to place — visible only when sought, but doing the silent structural work of making the form feel *carved* rather than drawn.

At the center lives the only event: three short horizontal bars, rendered in pure white at a single mathematically-derived weight. They are spaced according to a ratio chosen and abandoned a dozen times before settling. Each terminates in a softened cap — never sharp, never round, the specific geometry that whispers *crafted, not generated*. The negative space between bars is as deliberate as the bars themselves.

The palette is reduced to the absolute minimum: a charcoal field, a single white mark, the transparent void around the disc. Color has been refused as a discipline. The work argues that restraint is not absence — it is the most labored-over decision in the composition.

The intent: an object you can poke a thousand times and the thousandth feel as considered as the first. Every pixel placed by someone at the top of their craft, painstakingly tuned at 8× zoom until the form feels *inevitable*. Anti-aliased to glass. Aligned to integer pixels at the edges of the icon. The kind of detail-work that takes hours and reads in milliseconds.

It is a button. It is also a small, complete artifact.
